How we're getting around NZ

InterCity North & South Highlights 🚌

This is our New Zealand bus pass. Carrie and I already bought ours ✓, and the others still need to buy theirs. It goes one way, north to south, and includes the Wellington → Picton Interislander ferry. It covers a minimum of seven travel days, and it is not an unlimited nationwide pass.

Main pass cityStop along the way
Auckland→Rotorua→Taupō→Wellington⛴Picton→Nelson→Greymouth→Franz Josef→Fox Glacier→Wānaka→Queenstown

Other stops are allowed. InterCity says you can hop on and off at any other stop along the route, including the little towns in between, until that leg is finished. That's how Taupō, Fox Glacier and Wānaka work for us. We just can't go backward or repeat a section.

Our 14 nights

New Zealand: our bus route

Seven overnight stops along the pass route, with four nights in Queenstown at the end. The route is set: buses and the ferry all the way to Queenstown, with no flights until Queenstown → Melbourne. What we do in each place can still change. Teal cards are main pass cities, and the dashed card is a stop along the way.

Why this version? Fewer one-night stops and fewer luggage changes, two nights in Franz Josef instead of three, and four nights in Queenstown. That gives us three full Queenstown days (Jan 15–17) after the Jan 14 arrival, and Milford Sound can move between days if the weather is bad.

The long Nelson → Franz Josef day is on purpose. There's nothing at Greymouth or Hokitika that beats our main stops, so we'd rather do one big travel day and keep the rest as full days.

Places we pass through

Taupō: Huka Falls and Tongariro Alpine Crossing are great, but they need more time than a quick stop. We're saving the big hiking days for the South Island.

Picton: The ferry through the Marlborough Sounds is scenic on its own. Going on to Nelson gives us a full Abel Tasman day instead of another hotel change.

Greymouth: A bus connection on the way to Franz Josef. The big sights nearby (Punakaiki, Hokitika Gorge, TranzAlpine) need extra travel, so skipping an overnight gives us more Queenstown time.

Travel and luggage

Our stays are all in towns on the InterCity network, so we can walk to most from the bus stop, or split a short taxi in Wellington. For day trips we leave big bags at our stay and come back the same evening. A separate Tekapo overnight is the main luggage problem.

The big travel day

Nelson → Franz Josef (9h 49m)

The portal shows this as one trip, but it's two buses:

LegTimesBus
Nelson Hub → Greymouth Railway Station7:15 AM–1:15 PM (6h)InterCity IC8825
Greymouth → Franz Josef Bus Stop1:30–5:04 PM (3h 34m)GreatSights GS9829

That means one real bus change in Greymouth, with only 15 minutes to make it. We'd book it as one connected journey, not as two separate legs, so the connection is protected. We also don't want to experiment with how the pass counts legs until we've checked.

What about bathroom and food breaks? "Direct service" only means no bus change. It doesn't mean nonstop, and InterCity's help pages say direct buses can stop along the way, with meal and rest breaks marked in the full timetable. Regular InterCity coaches don't have toilets, and most GreatSights coaches do (not guaranteed). In the timetable, a knife-and-fork icon means a refreshment stop, and a star means the bus only stops if someone booked to get on or off there. We couldn't confirm where the breaks are on our January date, so we still need to click SHOW ALL STOPS on the 7:15 AM bus.

Optional idea · not built into our route

Lake Tekapo + Aoraki/Mount Cook + Hooker Valley

These destinations are in the same broad alpine region, so combining Tekapo and a Mount Cook hike may make more sense than treating them as two separate Queenstown day trips. Whether we do either one is still open.

Idea 1 · Queenstown-based excursion

Keep all four Queenstown nights and leave large luggage at our accommodation. Explore a guided Mount Cook / Hooker Valley day tour, ideally with a Tekapo stop if the route and timing allow. A full Hooker Valley hike needs dedicated walking time; not every sightseeing tour provides it.

Trade-off: long road travel (roughly 3–4 hours each way to Mount Cook before stops/hiking) and less time in Queenstown. A quick Tekapo stop is different from an overnight with stargazing.

Idea 2 · Separate overnight excursion

Consider a one-night Tekapo/Mount Cook side trip from Wānaka or Queenstown. This may offer more hiking time or stargazing, but adds transport and possibly an extra accommodation booking.

Luggage question: if we keep our original room, ask whether it can hold large bags while we travel with daypacks; otherwise we need to carry luggage on separate transport and arrange storage. Wānaka ↔ Tekapo is not covered by the one-way InterCity pass and public connections may be awkward.

Still to investigate: current Hooker Valley Track/bridge access on DOC, guided-tour walking time, Tekapo stops, total travel time, extra cost, and what we would give up in Queenstown. None of this is reserved. A third option is to skip the detour and spend that day around Queenstown/Glenorchy.

Allie's ideas ✨

Everything in these cards is from my list. They depend on the nights we end up spending in each place, and a few need a tour or shuttle from the bus stop.

Our South Island ideas lean toward alpine mountains, lakes, glaciers and beaches. Difficulty ratings are general guides; weather, trail status and transport matter. Images are illustrative destination references, and we can swap activities based on the group's interests.

Auckland · Jan 5 · booked by Allie

Hobbiton tour

I booked mine through GreatSights (early pickup, back around 3:30–3:45 PM, full refund if I cancel by Jan 4). Others can book too, or have a free day in Auckland.

Rotorua · popular ideas

Geothermal parks & Māori culture

Te Puia geyser and Māori arts; Wai-O-Tapu colorful mineral pools; Māori-led evening experiences at Mitai or Te Pā Tū; Redwoods Treewalk and hot pools. We only have the afternoon of Jan 6 and part of Jan 7, so we can pick one or two.

Nelson · full-day option

Abel Tasman coast

Easy–moderate depending on section. Beaches, water taxi, kayaking and short coastal hikes; requires a shuttle/tour from Nelson.

Franz Josef · full-day option

Glacier valley & Lake Matheson

Easy–moderate walks depending on trail. Lake Matheson is near Fox Glacier and needs separate transport; guided glacier activities depend on weather and cost.

Wānaka · hard

Roys Peak · hard / strenuous

About 16 km return, 5–6 hours, steep with more than 1,200 m of climbing. Famous Lake Wānaka panorama; taxi/shuttle to trailhead. An idea for our full Wānaka day.

Wānaka · easier alternative

Mount Iron · easy–moderate

About 4–5 km loop, usually 1.5–2 hours. Great lake views with much less effort than Roys Peak.

Aoraki/Mount Cook · optional

Hooker Valley · easy–moderate

Around 10 km return and 3–4 hours if the full route is open; dramatic glacier valley and mountain views. Separate long excursion from Queenstown or Tekapo; check DOC access and tour walking time.

Fiordland · optional day hike

Routeburn / Key Summit · moderate

Key Summit is roughly 6.8 km return, ~3 hours from The Divide. Alpine views; transport from Queenstown or a suitable guided trip required. The full Routeburn Track is a multi-day trek, not our proposed day hike.

Queenstown · local hiking

Queenstown Hill / Ben Lomond

Queenstown Hill: moderate, roughly 2–3 hours return. Ben Lomond summit: hard, a longer full mountain day. Both are alternatives to another long-distance tour.

Queenstown · excursion

Milford Sound cruise

Scenic coach-and-cruise usually takes a full day from Queenstown (often 12–13 hours). We're aiming for Sat Jan 16, with Jan 15 or 17 as weather backups. Not booked, and not included in the InterCity pass.

Other popular ideas

Waitomo, Huka Falls, Tongariro, Glenorchy

Waitomo glowworm caves need a separate tour; Huka Falls/Tongariro need dedicated time around Taupō; Glenorchy has scenery and access to Routeburn walks. These are options, not extra stops we have to squeeze in.
